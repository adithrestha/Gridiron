Using Predictions

  • Pick a week along the top. Games are sorted from most to least confident.
  • Each card shows the predicted winner and win chance, the predicted final score, and both quarterbacks. The bar fills toward the favorite.
  • Model line vs Vegas: the model's point spread next to the betting line. Edge is the gap between them; 3 points or more is highlighted. The model never sees the Vegas line, so the edge is a genuine disagreement.
  • Flags call out a starting QB ruled out, a team coming off its bye, or a short week.
  • Projected final records add each team's current record to its win chances in every remaining game.

Using Fantasy

  1. Choose your scoring (PPR, half PPR or standard) and the week at the top of the tab.
  2. Add your teams. Signed in, connect a Sleeper username or ESPN league ID and your rosters arrive shortly after. You can also paste a roster into the lineup helper and save it, with or without an account.
  3. Read My teams. Each team shows its record and every week's score from your league, a week-by-week breakdown of what each player scored against what the model projected beforehand (and what the model's lineup would have scored), then the best lineup for this week and the free agents worth adding and who to drop for them. Pickups weigh projected rest-of-season points and how widely each player is rostered across ESPN leagues. Players rostered in at least half of leagues and widely rostered injured players are never suggested as drops. 1st- and 2nd-round picks are protected too, unless their playing time is bad: after 3 or more games this season, under 40% of offensive snaps or under 5 half-PPR points a game over their last 4. Drop suggestions show that snap share and scoring.
  4. Browse the projections by position, or search a player. Proj is what he scores if he plays. Risk adj multiplies that by his chance of playing. Rest of season adds up every remaining week. Under each name is his projected stat line.

Models and data

Predictions engine
  • Data: nflverse play-by-play summaries: every game result and Vegas line since 1999, weekly team and player stats since 2012, official injury reports, snap counts, depth charts and rosters.
  • Team strength: Elo ratings since 1999 and recent form in points and EPA (expected points added per play), adjusted for opponent strength and split into passing and rushing.
  • Quarterbacks: every QB rated on EPA per play and completion % over expected; the projected starter comes from the depth chart and injury report.
  • Situation: rest days, byes, travel miles, time zones and body-clock kickoff time, home field, division games, roof, surface and weather.
  • Injuries: each injured player weighted by position and snap share.
  • Models: eight machine-learning models (logistic regression, random forest, extra trees, gradient boosting, histogram boosting, a neural net, SVM and k-nearest neighbors) blended by a stacking model. Separate stacked models predict the margin and total points.
Fantasy projections
  • Volume: pass attempts, carries and targets per game, weighted toward recent games and nudged by the trend in snap share.
  • Efficiency: yards, catches and touchdowns per opportunity, pulled toward the position average so a few long plays don't get projected forward.
  • Game script: the team's expected points from the predictions engine, blended with the Vegas line for the current week.
  • Quarterback: receivers move with the engine's rating of this week's starter.
  • Matchup: the opponent's opponent-adjusted pass and run defense, and what it allows to each position on runs and on passes (carries, yards per carry, targets, catches, yards per target). Man vs zone coverage and splits against top-5 defenses are shown on the Fantasy tab but not used, because they didn't improve accuracy in testing.
  • Availability: injury designations set each player's chance of sitting, and part of an absent starter's work goes to his teammates.

Both are tested on past weeks, each predicted using only what was known before it. The numbers at the top of this tab come from those tests. The predictions engine still picks winners slightly less often than simply taking the Vegas favorite.

When it updates

  • Predictions: fully retrained with fresh data at 7:41 AM ET on every game day and the day before.
  • Fantasy: refreshed every morning at 8:13 AM ET with new stats, injury reports, depth charts and game projections. League rosters refresh at the same time, and new leagues import within minutes to an hour of being added.

How it backtests

Each period was predicted by a model trained only on earlier seasons. Against the spread (ATS) needs 52.4% to break even at standard −110 odds.

The first report runs the Tuesday morning after Monday Night Football.

How grading works

  • Graded against what was published. Every refresh saves a copy of the picks, fantasy projections and Kalshi board for games that haven't kicked off. The last copy before each kickoff is the one graded. Week 8 was rebuilt from the copies that survived (the engine's Thursday run, the Friday fantasy and Kalshi boards, and Monday's for the night game).
  • Game picks are compared with the Vegas favorite and with Vegas win chances from the closing moneylines. Brier score is the average squared miss on the win chance; lower is better, and a coin flip scores 0.25.
  • Fantasy grades every player projected at least 5 half-PPR points (kickers and defenses aren't graded). A player who didn't play counts as 0. The comparison guess is each player's season average before that week.
  • Betting settles every saved Kalshi market from Kalshi's own result. "Model" is the chance shown on the Betting tab (after pulling toward Kalshi); "Kalshi" is the market's mid price. Value bets and top picks are scored as one contract each, bought at the ask plus Kalshi's fee.
  • One week is noisy. A 16-game week can swing 15 points of accuracy by luck alone; trust the season-to-date numbers more.

Read this first. The game model is about as accurate as Vegas, not better: against the spread it went 51–53% in backtests, under the 52.4% needed to profit at standard odds. Kalshi's game prices track Vegas closely, so a big gap between the model and Kalshi usually means the model is missing news (an injury, a role change, a depth-chart move).

So every probability here is pulled most of the way back toward Kalshi's price before it's compared, and a market is only flagged when it still clears the price plus Kalshi's fee. Player props were tested against 20,140 settled Kalshi props from 2026 weeks 3–8, using Kalshi's price at kickoff. On its own the model is less accurate than Kalshi; pulled 75% of the way toward Kalshi it is slightly more accurate. Following these picks (one per player and stat) would have made +12% on 257 bets, but the plausible range runs from −4% to +28%, so that isn't proven yet. Pass-yard props are never picked: the model made Kalshi's pass-yard prices worse.

Kalshi prices haven't been pulled yet. They refresh at about 8, 12 and 4 ET on game days.

      Projections

      Defense matchups

      Yards each defense allows per game to each position, on runs and on passes, ranked 1st (fewest allowed) to 32nd (most), weighted toward recent games. Green is a soft matchup, red a tough one. Click a column to sort.

      How it works

      • Volume: pass attempts, carries and targets per game, weighted toward recent games, nudged by the trend in each player's snap share.
      • Efficiency: yards, catches and touchdowns per opportunity, pulled toward the position average so a few long scores don't get projected forward.
      • Game script: each team's expected points come from the prediction engine, which already accounts for Elo, rest, travel and body clock, team form, injuries and the starting QB. This week also blends in the Vegas line.
      • Quarterback: receivers and tight ends move with the engine's rating of this week's likely starter compared with the QBs they've been catching passes from.
      • Matchup: the engine's opponent-adjusted pass and run defense numbers, plus what the opponent allows to each position stat by stat: carries and yards per carry, targets, catches and yards per target. So a defense that shuts down RB runs but leaks RB catches moves a pass-catching back differently from a pure runner.
      • Shown but not used: man vs zone coverage and splits against top-5 defenses appear in the Matchup column. Tested on past weeks, neither made projections more accurate. A player's man vs zone split barely carries from one season to the next, and his split against top-5 defenses doesn't carry at all.
      • Availability: injury designations (or practice reports before Friday) set the chance a player sits. When a regular is out, part of his targets and carries go to his healthy teammates.
      • Kickers and defenses use standard scoring: FG 3/4/5 points by distance, sacks, takeaways, defensive TDs and points-allowed tiers.

      Not modeled: weather, and rookies or new starters with no NFL games yet.
